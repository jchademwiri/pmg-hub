Winning government and state-owned entity (SOE) tenders in South Africa can transform an emerging SME into an industry player. However, over **70% of tender bids are disqualified during the preliminary administrative evaluation stage** before the pricing or technical evaluation even begins.

Administrative non-compliance is the single greatest reason tender proposals fail. In this guide, we break down the mandatory returnables and compliance essentials you must have in place to ensure your bids are never disqualified at gate zero.

---

## 1. Central Supplier Database (CSD) Registration

Your **Central Supplier Database (CSD)** registration with National Treasury is the foundation of any public sector bid.

- **Unique Registration Number (URN)**: All bids require your active `MAAA...` supplier number.
- **Verification Status**: Your CSD profile must show green ticks across verified bank account details, active directors (cross-referenced with CIPC), and real-time SARS tax status.
- **Master CSD Report**: Always download a fresh, updated Master CSD summary report dated within 7 days of your tender submission deadline.

---

## 2. SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) Pin

Public procurement regulations strictly prohibit state organs from awarding contracts to non-compliant taxpayers.

- Keep your tax status compliant across **Income Tax, PAYE (if employing staff), and VAT**.
- Request an active **Tax Compliance Pin** from SARS eFiling.
- Ensure the pin does not expire while the bid evaluation committee is adjudicating bids.

---

## 3. B-BBEE Sworn Affidavit vs. SANAS Certificate

Preference points systems under the Preferential Procurement Policy Framework Act (PPPFA) directly impact your final score (80/20 or 90/10 preference point systems).

- **Exempted Micro Enterprises (EMEs)** (Annual turnover under R10 million): Submit a valid, signed and stamped **DTI Sworn Affidavit** completed in front of a Commissioner of Oaths. Common disqualification trap: using an outdated affidavit template or missing the financial year-end date.
- **Qualifying Small Enterprises (QSEs)** (Annual turnover between R10 million and R50 million): Must provide a formal certificate issued by a **SANAS-accredited** verification agency unless 51%+ black-owned.

---

## 4. CIDB Contractor Grading (Infrastructure & Construction)

If you are bidding on construction, maintenance, electrical, or civil engineering projects:

- Register with the **Construction Industry Development Board (CIDB)**.
- Grades range from **1 (up to R500,000)** up to **9 (unlimited contract value)** across classes such as **GB (General Building)**, **CE (Civil Engineering)**, and **ME (Mechanical Engineering)**.
- Ensure your track record, track record certificates of completion, and financial statements are submitted for grade advancement.

---

## 5. Standard Bidding Documents (SBD Returnables)

Every National and Provincial department tender includes mandatory SBD schedules:

1. **SBD 1**: Invitation to Bid and terms of submission.
2. **SBD 4**: Bidder's Disclosure (replaces the older SBD 8 and SBD 9). Discloses any potential conflict of interest, state employment, or collusion.
3. **SBD 6.1**: Preference Points Claim Form for specific goals under the 2022 preferential procurement regulations.
